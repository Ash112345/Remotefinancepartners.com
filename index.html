It is not the people, the software or the trade

In a warehouse the barcode makes the record a by-product of the movement. The scan is how the pick gets confirmed, so the record is created by the person doing the job, at the moment they do it.

A pack of timber gets broken down at the kerb. A wrapped pallet of bags has no barcode. Bulk fuel is a quantity rather than an object. Feed and fertiliser go out by the tonne. A hire item leaves on a nod and comes back when it comes back. So somebody writes down later what they think went out, and your accounts are built on top of that.

Most owners arrive with one of those three theories. It is usually none of them. Your team are solving problems in real time with the information they have. The business grew but the way it records itself did not.

You already know the symptoms

  • The same delivery goes out twice. The first one never made it onto the system, so someone loads it again. You paid for the stock, the driver and the fuel, twice.
  • Stock moves between branches and nobody writes it down. Right call on the day. Three weeks later a branch is short with nothing but an argument to explain it.
  • The count never agrees, and nobody can tell you whether it is theft, breakage, a mis-keyed delivery or a transfer that was never recorded.
  • Every site writes up a delivery note its own way, and nobody has put the versions side by side.
  • You are still doing the numbers on a Sunday night.

People, process, platform. In that order.

People

Three people record the same delivery three different ways, one person just knows what is in the yard, and nobody is accountable for the numbers agreeing. We map who actually touches stock, cash and invoices, then say who does what, who checks it, and who carries it if it is wrong.

Process

We trace transactions from order to delivery to invoice to bank, and find where information is re-keyed, delayed or lost. Then we write it down in plain language, two sides of A4 per process. If it does not fit on that, your team will not follow it.

Platform

Last, not first. Nine times out of ten the answer is the system you already own, configured properly. Where a gap is genuinely a tooling gap we will say so and tell you what it costs. Where it is not, we will tell you that too.

What good looks like

Every load recorded once, before it leaves. Transfers between sites logged as they happen, on both sides. Stock, cash and invoices that agree. A named owner against every check, and a routine your team runs without you standing over it.

None of that requires new software or a new hire. It requires the recording to happen where the work happens, rather than afterwards from memory.

What this looks like in practice

A ten yard distributor. Stable, profitable, with a four person back office team and a daily routine that only one of them could run.

What we found

  • Revenue was a residual rather than a record. Sales were inferred from a nightly count, so no variance could ever arise.
  • Transfers between sites were put through as sales and returns, because no transfer route existed. Stock moving between two of the company's own yards looked identical to stock leaving the business.
  • Notes were being written in seven different formats across ten yards, so nothing could be checked automatically.
  • 54 activities mapped. 15 with no owner at all. 12 resting on one individual.

The tolerances, the discount ceiling and the four steps for tracing a missing pallet all existed and all worked. They existed in one head.

What we did

We shadowed the daily routine end to end before changing any of it, then wrote a standard operating procedure the yards actually work to: one recording convention, one unit of account, a standard yard sheet, a daily cut-off, and the judgements written down alongside the steps.

Ten yards, standardised, without pretending they are identical. One exchanges empties on its own arrangement, one handles no pallets, one closes two days a week. The procedure names those, so a variation is a documented exception rather than a site quietly doing its own thing.

No new software. We built a file that records a transfer as a transfer, with both ends of the movement on it, because the system had no route for one.

What we run now

We are embedded as their Quality and Operations function: daily reconciliation, standards and conventions, stock count oversight, exception management, documentation, data quality and reporting. Eight measures were baselined at the start and are reported monthly.

The routine now runs to a written standard rather than from one person's memory, and more than one person can run it. Exceptions get logged, assigned and chased instead of surfacing three weeks later.
