What this looks like in practice

A ten-yard distributor. Stable, profitable, with a four-person back office and a daily routine only one of them could run.

15with no owner

What we found

  • Sales were inferred from a nightly count, so no variance could ever show up.
  • Transfers between sites were put through as sales and returns, so stock moving between two of the company's own yards looked identical to stock leaving the business.
  • Notes were written in seven different formats across ten yards.

What we did

We shadowed the daily routine end to end before changing anything, then wrote a procedure the yards actually work to: one recording convention, a standard yard sheet, a daily cut-off, and the judgement calls written down alongside the steps.

No new software. Just a simple file that records a transfer as a transfer.

Where it is now

The routine runs to a written standard, and more than one person can run it.

We now provide their daily reconciliation, exception management and reporting, with eight measures baselined at the start and reported monthly.
