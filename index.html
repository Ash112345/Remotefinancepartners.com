Recent case study

A ten-yard distributor in Northern Ireland.

Stable and profitable, with a back office of four people. Only one of them could run the daily routine.

What we found

54activities

We mapped the 54 activities that keep this distributor running, day to day and month to month.

  • Sales were inferred from a nightly count, so no variance could ever show up.
  • Transfers between sites were put through as sales and returns, so stock moving between two of the company's own yards looked identical to stock leaving the business.
  • Notes were written in seven different formats across ten yards.
Book your audit
What we found

27.8%had no owner

15 of those 54 activities had nobody responsible for them. More than one in four of the jobs keeping the business alive were done by whoever noticed, or not at all.

We shadowed the daily routine end to end before changing anything. Then we wrote a procedure the yards actually work to: one recording convention, a standard yard sheet, a daily cut-off, and the judgement calls written down alongside the steps.

No new software. Just a simple file that records a transfer as a transfer.

What we found

22.2%rested on one person

A further 12 activities depended on a single member of staff. If they were off sick, on holiday or handed in their notice, nobody else could pick up the work.

The routine now runs to a written standard, and more than one person can run it.

We now provide their daily reconciliation, exception management and reporting, with eight measures baselined at the start and reported monthly.
